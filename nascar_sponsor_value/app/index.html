How the model works and what it assumes

Race data: NASCAR's public loop data and results for all 36 points races of 2025 (1,369 starts). Audiences: published Nielsen figures per race. Coverage is allocated across the field by average running position and laps led, calibrated so the top 10 cars take about half of coverage, the share Relo Metrics reports. Laps led carry extra weight, following Rotthoff, Depken and Groothuis (Applied Economics, 2014), who found leading, not winning, drives time on camera.

Value is ad-equivalent: clear seconds divided by 30, times the cost of a 30-second spot (CPM × audience). The Daytona 500 CPM is anchored to reported 2025 FOX spot prices of $400K–$500K. This is media value, not ROI. Return on the sponsorship depends on sales, hospitality and business-to-business value that broadcast data cannot see.

Every assumption below is drawn at random within its range in each simulation, so the spread shown is the uncertainty in the assumptions, not only in the race.

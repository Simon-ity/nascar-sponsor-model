# No. 44 sponsor value sheet (draft for discussion)

*Prepared independently from public 2025 data. Estimates, not contract terms. Built to be tested with people who sell and buy NASCAR sponsorships.*

## What one race delivers on TV

Primary-sponsor broadcast value of a typical No. 44 start: bottom quarter of the field, no laps led, no crash. Median and 80% range from the 2025 model.

| Broadcast window | 2025 races | Median audience | Value per start | 80% range | Clear logo time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FOX (broadcast) | 4 | 4.4M | **$158K** | $94K–$263K | ~38 s |
| FS1 | 8 | 2.5M | **$84K** | $49K–$142K | ~37 s |
| NBC (broadcast) | 4 | 2.7M | **$77K** | $45K–$132K | ~35 s |
| Prime Video | 5 | 2.1M | **$73K** | $43K–$123K | ~41 s |
| TNT | 5 | 2.1M | **$63K** | $36K–$111K | ~34 s |
| USA | 10 | 1.5M | **$55K** | $32K–$93K | ~36 s |

The Daytona 500 sits above all of these: about **$361K** if the car makes the field.

## Three pricing moves

**1. Price by window, not flat.** The car gets about the same seconds on screen everywhere. The audience changes, so the value changes almost threefold between a FOX broadcast race and a USA race. A single per-race rate either leaves money on the table in February or overprices September.

**2. Build the schedule around the FOX window.** The first 12 races (FOX and FS1) held about half of a backmarker's season broadcast value in 2025. Spring superspeedway and FOX broadcast races (Daytona, Atlanta, COTA, Talladega) are the strongest inventory.

**3. Sell the Daytona 500 with a make-good in writing.** Open cars made the 2025 Daytona 500 half the time (4 of 8 competing for spots). Expected value is about **$180K**, half the in-race value. Options that make the deal easier to sign:
- A credit toward a later FOX-window race (Atlanta or Talladega) if the car misses the show.
- A split fee: a base for Speedweeks presence (Duel coverage, garage, content) plus a bonus paid only if the car makes the 500.
- In 2025, open cars faced no qualification risk at any race with four or fewer open entries. Check the entry list before quoting any race; oversubscription is visible days ahead.

## What broadcast value does not cover

If a sponsor pays more per race than the numbers above, the difference has to come from things this model cannot see:
- **Hospitality and B2B:** garage access, suite nights, introductions to other sponsors in the paddock.
- **Content and social:** paint scheme reveals, driver content, the team's own channels.
- **The story:** NY Racing's HBCU schemes, NYC identity and minority ownership draw earned media that a standard exposure count misses.

The practical step is to track those for each race (impressions on social posts, number of hospitality guests, earned articles) so the next proposal can show them with the same rigor as the broadcast number.

## Method, briefly

2025 loop data and results for all 36 races, 2025 audiences by race, a coverage model calibrated to published research on how broadcasts distribute camera time, and 2,000 simulations across every assumption. Full method and sources: README.
